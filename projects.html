Passive Ankle Exoskeleton Research

2025 PRISE Fellow • Harvard Slade Lab

Conducted research as a 2025 PRISE Fellow at the Harvard Slade Lab under the mentorship of Jae-Ryeong Choi, working specifically on the design and development of a passive ankle exoskeleton to provide plantar flexion assistance during walking.

  • Designed and fabricated a novel shoe-binding ankle exoskeleton capable of providing plantar flexion torque assistance through a lightweight, non-invasive anchoring interface.
  • Utilized CAD, MATLAB, and Qualisys for design development and biomechanical analysis to compare and refine different iterations of the exoskeleton.

CAD Design

The main challenge in designing the exoskeleton interface was minimizing the amount of rigid components used in order to optimize usability and comfort. My initial CAD designs were modeled after the anatomy of the foot tripod; however, such a design limited adaptability. Thus, I eliminated the rigid component near the front of the foot and utilized a plantar fascia-inspired binding system to achieve a lightweight exoskeleton interface that had minimal interference when walking.

Early-stage Design of Exoskeleton

Design Evolution

In this stage of the design process, I combined a moment arm model from a previously researched rigid ankle exoskeleton in my lab with my initial CAD design in order to compare the torque transmission capabilities of the two devices. Through integrating the two models, I increased my proficiency in my CAD skills, building upon previously learned modeling techniques from my engineering courses.

Final Prototype

Following multiple design iterations, I presented my findings at the 2026 Harvard Summer Undergraduate Research Village (HSURV) poster presentation. The final prototype was completed shortly after.
