Shoe-Binding Ankle Exoskeleton

Harvard Slade Lab 2025 PRISE Fellow 2025 HCRP Fellow

Under the mentorship of Jae-Ryeong Choi, designed and developed a passive ankle exoskeleton to provide plantar flexion assistance during walking.

  • Designed and fabricated a novel shoe-binding ankle exoskeleton capable of providing plantar flexion torque assistance through a lightweight, non-invasive anchoring interface.
  • Utilized CAD, MATLAB, and Qualisys for design development and biomechanical analysis to compare and refine different iterations of the exoskeleton.

Turf Wars Competition

Cumulative project for Intro to Mechanical Engineering course (ES51) which consisted of a course-wide, team-based robotics competition. Challenges included maneuverability on both level and inclined surfaces, picking up and placing game pieces, and meeting size constraints. Utilized a lightweight, simple design consisting of a rotating "chomp" arm for item collection and storage. Won third place in the competition overall.

Skills: Mechanical design, CAD (Solidworks), 3D printing, CNC milling, laser cutting, molding, prototyping, mechanical assembly.

Egg Drop Competition

Final design challenge for my Mechanical Systems course which involved designing a carrier capable of withstanding a 5 ft minimum drop through applying knowledge of mass-spring-damper systems. Additional design criteria included minimizing the weight and height of the egg carrier, along with the drop acceleration. The provided shocks had a maximum compression of around 1.3 inches. My team utilized square plates with cutouts to reduce weight, and simulated the different shocks and springs in order to determine the optimal location for each spring and damper. Our final device was successfully able to withstand a 5ft drop.

Skills: Mechanical design, CAD, structural analysis, prototyping, 3D printing, laser cutting, and assembly.

Bridge Design Project

A fun design competition for my Mechanics of Solids course that allowed us to explore stress concentrations/failure hands-on. Material selection ranged between plywood and acrylic sheets, and bridge assembly could not include fasteners with the exception of moderate amounts of glue. My team utilized a deck truss arch design with increased surface area at points of contact with the Instron, adding cutouts wherever possible to minimize weight while ensuring structural integrity.

Skills: Mechanical design, CAD, laser cutting, and assembly.

EchoTap

Final project for Intro to Electrical Engineering (ES50) course that combined both digital and analog circuitry into a wearable electronic device to alert hard-of-hearing users of noises (and thereby potential dangers) in their surrounding environment. (Skills learned: working with analog/digital electronics and microcontrollers.)

Skills: Analog electronics, digital electronics, microcontrollers, circuit design, PCB assembly.
